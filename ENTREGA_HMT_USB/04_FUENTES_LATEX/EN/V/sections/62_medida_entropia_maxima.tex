\subsection{Stationary distribution and variational characterization}
\label{v:subsec:parry-explicita}

Let \(X_{\rm av}\) be the space of two-sided sequences over
\(\{\mathrm{ar},\mathrm{vol}\}\) whose consecutive pairs have
positive incidence in \(F_{\rm av}\). It is the one-step-memory envelope
of the mode calendar: it retains the admissible pairs while allowing
all concatenations compatible with them. Its stationary
measure weights that space of histories; the chronological frequency
of the orbit \((\Sigma,\Pi,\Pi)\) remains, separately,
\((1/3,2/3)\).

\begin{proposition}[Explicit characterization of the measure of maximal entropy]
\label{v:prop:parry-explicita}
With \(r=(1,\varphi)\), the stochastic matrix and its stationary
distribution are
\begin{equation}
 P_{ij}=\frac{(F_{\rm av})_{ij}r_j}{\varphi r_i},\qquad
 P=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\\varphi^{-2}&\varphi^{-1}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \pi_{\rm av}=\frac{(1,\varphi^2)}{1+\varphi^2}.
 \label{v:eq:parry-transicion-estacionaria}
\end{equation}
The stationary Markov measure \(\mu\) determined by these data
assigns to each admissible cylinder of \(n\) transitions
\begin{equation}
 \mu[i_0\ldots i_n]
 =\frac{r_{i_0}r_{i_n}}{(1+\varphi^2)\varphi^n}.
 \label{v:eq:parry-cilindros}
\end{equation}
It is the unique stationary measure on \(X_{\rm av}\) with maximal
entropy rate, and that rate is \(\log\varphi\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identity \(\varphi^2=\varphi+1\) implies
\(\varphi^{-2}+\varphi^{-1}=1\), so the rows of \(P\)
sum to one. Direct substitution verifies
\(\pi_{\rm av}P=\pi_{\rm av}\). Both cross-transitions
have positive probability; the chain is irreducible, and the self-transition
at \(\mathrm{vol}\) makes it aperiodic. The stationary distribution
is also obtained by solving the two linear equations and the
normalization, which give the unique solution displayed above. Multiplication of
\(\pi_{i_0}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}P_{i_k i_{k+1}}\) cancels the intermediate
factors \(r_{i_k}\) and proves \eqref{v:eq:parry-cilindros}.

For maximality, let \(\nu\) be any stationary measure
on \(X_{\rm av}\), and let
\(q(j\mid\mathcal P)\) be the conditional distribution of \(X_1\)
given the entire past \(\mathcal P=(\ldots,X_{-1},X_0)\).
Its support is contained in that of the row \(P_{X_0,\cdot}\).
For finite distributions, the relative divergence
\(D(q\Vert p)=\sum_jq_j\log(q_j/p_j)\) is nonnegative and
vanishes exactly when \(q=p\). This property follows from
concavity of the logarithm, with the convention \(0\log0=0\).
On admissible pairs,
\[
 \log P_{ij}=\log r_j-\log r_i-\log\varphi.
\]
Stationarity cancels the expectation of
\(\log r_{X_1}-\log r_{X_0}\). Hence
\begin{equation}
 h(\nu)=H_\nu(X_1\mid\mathcal P)
 =\log\varphi-
 \mathbb E_\nu D\!\left(q(\cdot\mid\mathcal P)
                  \Vert P_{X_0,\cdot}\right)
 \leq\log\varphi.
 \label{v:eq:parry-deficit-entropia}
\end{equation}
The identity between entropy rate and entropy conditional on the past follows
from the chain rule on finite blocks and the decreasing limit
of their conditional entropies; the finite alphabet ensures
that all of them lie between zero and \(\log2\).

The constructed measure \(\mu\) is Markov with transition matrix \(P\),
so the divergence term vanishes and
\(h(\mu)=\log\varphi\). If another stationary measure attains
that rate, the divergence vanishes almost surely: its conditional
distribution of the future depends only on \(X_0\) and agrees with \(P\).
It is therefore the same stationary chain, whose unique initial
distribution is \(\pi_{\rm av}\). This proves uniqueness.
\end{proof}

Formula \eqref{v:eq:parry-deficit-entropia} specifies exactly what is maximized:
entropy per transition of the stated symbolic envelope. The ratio
\(\mu_{\rm ar}/\mu_{\rm vol}=\varphi^{-2}\) arises from its stationary
weights. The periodic chronological orbit has zero entropy
rate and retains its own temporal distribution; both objects remain
defined on their respective domains.
