\subsection{Coupling direction and unimodular flow}

The extraction of the register, its rational reading, and the incidence projectors have been constructed in the preceding sections. The base-thousand convention, phase origin, carry boundary, and chart of twelve positions are retained. In that chart,
\[
 K=(234,543,140,729,659,824,621,058,914,794,146,601),
 \qquad u=P_3P_{11}K,
\]
\[
 P_{11}=I-\frac1{12}\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 u\perp\mathbf1,\qquad
 \|u\|^2=\frac{6638585+2275584\sqrt5}{20}>0.
\]
The finite definition of \(P_3\), the order of its classes, and the exact evaluation of this norm are given in Lemma~\ref{lem:k-sector-no-nulo}. The following realization uses that generated object. We write
\begin{equation}
 \rho=\frac{\pi_{\HMT}}{\varphi_{\HMT}^2}>0,\qquad
 h_i=(\log\rho)\frac{u_i}{\|u\|},\qquad
 r_i(t)=e^{th_i},\qquad g_t=\operatorname{diag}(r_i(t)).
 \label{rec:eq:flujo}
\end{equation}
The regional coordinates are evaluated before this composition. The parameter \(t\in\RR\) indexes a mathematical deformation; its definition identifies it with neither the TPK clock nor a refinement depth.

\begin{proposition}[Scale compensation]
\label{rec:prop:flujo}
The flow satisfies
\[
 g_tg_s=g_{t+s},\qquad g_0=I,\qquad g_t^{-1}=g_{-t},\qquad
 \det g_t=\prod_i r_i(t)=1.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Orthogonality to the uniform mode gives \(\sum_i h_i=0\). Multiplication of exponentials proves the group law and inversion. The determinant is \(\exp(t\sum_i h_i)=1\). Every diagonal factor is positive.
\end{proof}

The conservation law is multiplicative and global: each radius may vary. In logarithmic coordinates, the vanishing sum is preserved. This distinction will provide the geometric interpretation of the transverse projector of rank ten.

\subsection{Exact reciprocity of the double circle}
\label{rec:doble-circulo}

Consider the complex chart and its inverse
\[
 z(s)=\frac{s-1}{s},\qquad s(z)=\frac1{1-z}.
\]
For \(r>0\), \(z=re^{i\theta}\ne1\), define \(s(r,\theta)=(1-re^{i\theta})^{-1}\).

\begin{proposition}[Conjugacy of the spectral reflection]
\label{rec:prop:espejo}
The following identities hold:
\begin{align}
 s(r^{-1},\theta)&=1-\overline{s(r,\theta)},\label{rec:eq:espejo}\\
 \Re s(r,\theta)-\frac12&=
 \frac{1-r^2}{2|1-re^{i\theta}|^2}.\label{rec:eq:lado}
\end{align}
The unit circle, apart from the excluded point \(z=1\), corresponds to the line \(\Re s=1/2\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Radial inversion at fixed angle is \(z'=1/\overline z\). Hence
\[
 \frac1{1-z'}=\frac{\overline z}{\overline z-1}
 =1-\frac1{1-\overline z}.
\]
Moreover, \((1-z)^{-1}=(1-\overline z)/|1-z|^2\). Subtracting \(1/2\) from its real part gives \eqref{rec:eq:lado}.
\end{proof}

The relation to the radii of \eqref{rec:eq:flujo} is explicit: \(r_i(-t)=r_i(t)^{-1}\). Equality of products preserves volume, whereas the proposition identifies an involution between complex charts. Locating the zeros of a function requires the properties of that function; it does not follow from the unit product of radii.

Three circular realizations in the corpus should be distinguished. The spectral chart maps \(s=1/2+i\gamma\) to \(z_\gamma=(\gamma+i/2)/(\gamma-i/2)\). The dual circle of integer memory maps an integer \(n\) to the character \(z^n\). Nonadic compression maps \(z\) to \((8+z)/9\), a circle with centre \(8/9\) and radius \(1/9\), tangent to the unit circle at \(1\). The modal ratio \(5/9\), by contrast, belongs to the eigenvalues \(9,5\) of the APP block \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}7&2\\2&7\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Their functions remain distinct when they are composed.

\subsection{Positroid strata and balanced invariants}

Let \(X\) be a real matrix of rank \(k\), with twelve columns, and let \(\Delta_I(X)\) denote its maximal minor with columns \(I\). The right action satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_I(Xg_t)=e^{t\sum_{i\in I}h_i}\Delta_I(X).
 \label{rec:eq:plucker}
\end{equation}
Diagonal positivity preserves all signs and all vanishing minors. Thus, for \(1\le k\le11\), a trajectory starting in \(\operatorname{Gr}_{\ge0}(k,12)\) remains in the same positroid stratum. The stratification by minors is used here as a downstream realization; see Postnikov's general construction~\cite{rec:postnikov}.

\begin{proposition}[Positive duality of complementary ranks]
Let \(A_{\pm}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1,\ldots)\) and
\(\mathfrak D(V)=V^\perp A_{\pm}\), with the positive orientation of its complementary minors. Then
\[
 \mathfrak D(Vg_t)=\mathfrak D(V)g_{-t}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since \(g_t\) is diagonal and self-adjoint,
\((Vg_t)^\perp=V^\perp g_t^{-1}\). The matrix \(A_{\pm}\) commutes with \(g_t\). For the signs of the minors, orthogonal complementation introduces, up to a global sign, \((-1)^{\sum_{j\in J}j}\). Multiplication of the columns by \((-1)^{j-1}\) leaves a sign independent of \(J\); a single global orientation removes it. The complementary minors are then nonnegative.
\end{proof}

If \(\mathbf1_I+\mathbf1_J=\mathbf1_L+\mathbf1_M\) and the denominator is nonzero, coordinatewise cancellation of the exponents proves
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Delta_I(Xg_t)\Delta_J(Xg_t)}{\Delta_L(Xg_t)\Delta_M(Xg_t)}
 =\frac{\Delta_I(X)\Delta_J(X)}{\Delta_L(X)\Delta_M(X)}.
 \label{rec:eq:cocientes}
\end{equation}
In rank two this recovers the four-index ratios and their exchange relations. By contrast, the product of complementary minors of the two paired trajectories receives the same factor twice and may vary. Nor does this action make the circulant frame \(O_K\), which is invertible for recovery, totally nonnegative: its minors retain the signs established in its own realization.

\subsection{Covolume, dual lattice, and ternary symmetry}

Let \(\Lambda\) be the marked Leech lattice constructed earlier, self-dual and of covolume one in the metric of its twelve orthogonal planes of type \(A_2\). The marked isometry
\[
 \sigma=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{12}P_i\mathsf C P_i^{-1},\qquad
 \mathsf C^2+\mathsf C+I=0,
\]
preserves the gluing and \(\Lambda\), has order three, and has no nonzero fixed vectors. The \(P_i\) are plane frames, distinct from the projectors \(P_3,P_{11}\). Define
\begin{equation}
 G_t=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{12}r_i(t)I_{A_{2,i}},\qquad
 \Lambda_t=G_t\Lambda.
 \label{rec:eq:red}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Reciprocal lattice deformation]
\label{rec:thm:red}
For every \(t\in\RR\),
\[
 \operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_t)=1,\qquad
 \Lambda_t^*=\Lambda_{-t},\qquad \sigma\Lambda_t=\Lambda_t.
\]
Every isometry of \(\Lambda\) that commutes with \(G_t\) also preserves \(\Lambda_t\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each radius acts on two coordinates; hence
\(\det G_t=\prod_i r_i(t)^2=1\). For an invertible operator \(G\), a vector \(y\) belongs to \((G\Lambda)^*\) exactly when \(G^*y\in\Lambda^*\). Therefore
\((G\Lambda)^*=G^{-*}\Lambda^*\). Applying self-duality, self-adjointness, and inversion gives \(\Lambda_t^*=G_{-t}\Lambda\). Finally, \(G_t\) is scalar on each plane and commutes with \(\sigma\), whence
\(\sigma G_t\Lambda=G_t\sigma\Lambda=G_t\Lambda\). The same calculation applies to the other commuting isometries. The operator \(\sigma\) is unchanged, so it retains its order and its absence of fixed vectors.
\end{proof}

The point \(t=0\) recovers the original lattice together with its integrality, evenness, self-duality, and exceptional realization. The full collection consists of unimodular Euclidean lattices in the sense of covolume one; away from the original point, neither integrality nor a Moonshine realization is presumed. The theorem identifies precisely the ternary symmetry that persists and the duality relating the two directions of deformation.
