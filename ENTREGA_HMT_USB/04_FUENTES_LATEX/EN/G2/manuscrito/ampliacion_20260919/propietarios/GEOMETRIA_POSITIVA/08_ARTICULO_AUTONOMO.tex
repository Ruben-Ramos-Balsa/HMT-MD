\subsection{Carry, subdivision and ordered parameter}

\begin{equation}

 \label{geometria_positiva:eq:carry-map-positive}
\end{equation}
It preserves the rescaled sum, since
\[
 \frac{Bx-c}{B(x+y)}+\frac{By+c}{B(x+y)}=1.
\]
Its inverse recovers \(c\) through the declared residue class, then recovers \((x,y)\) by Euclidean division. By composition, a carry word \(c_1\ldots c_n\) determines a cell of depth \(n\), and divisions in reverse order recover the complete word. \subsection{Canonical forms and cancellation of interior boundaries}

 \label{geometria_positiva:eq:box-canonical-form}
\end{equation}

\label{geometria_positiva:thm:tpk-higher-residues} Subdivide each coordinate interval by the TPK refinement points, and denote the resulting cuboids, with induced orientation, by \(Q_{\mathbf j}\). Then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle

 \label{geometria_positiva:eq:box-telescopy}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Res}_{F_s}\cdots\operatorname{Res}_{F_1}\Omega_Q
 =\operatorname{sgn}(F_\bullet)\,\Omega_{F_s}.
 \label{geometria_positiva:eq:iterated-residue}
\end{equation}
In particular, the residue at each vertex has modulus one and the sign fixed by orientation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The sum over the grid is the exterior product of the \(d\) one-dimensional sums. Applying \eqref{geometria_positiva:eq:interval-telescopy} in each factor gives \eqref{geometria_positiva:eq:box-telescopy}. An interior facet has two incident cells and opposite normal orientations, so their residues cancel. Taking a residue removes the corresponding normal factor and leaves the face's canonical form with its orientation sign. Iteration proves \eqref{geometria_positiva:eq:iterated-residue}.
