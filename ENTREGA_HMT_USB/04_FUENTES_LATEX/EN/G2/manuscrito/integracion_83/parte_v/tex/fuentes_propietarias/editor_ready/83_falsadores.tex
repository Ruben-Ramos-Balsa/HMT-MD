
This chapter gathers the negative controls for the five closures. A falsifier invalidates only the
arrow and domain that it targets; it does not transfer its failure to another initial structure.
Likewise, having a complete structure does not replace the specific proof
of its application.

\section{Separation between structure and application}

For each $T$, $\mathfrak G_T$ is the complete view that the intrinsic operation
$\mathfrak O_T$ extracts without restricting the single common record.
Its existence is an assertion distinct from the existence of the final reader
$\mathcal P_T:\mathfrak M_T\to\mathcal C_T$. A failure in $\mathcal P_T$ does not
erase $\mathfrak G_T$; the existence of $\mathfrak G_T$ likewise does not by
itself prove $\mathcal P_T$.

\section{Conditions of existence, uniqueness, and compatibility of terminal publications}

No application chain is considered proved by a declaration or an isolated numerical result. Promotion requires: a formula for the map; domains and codomains; proof that it commutes with differentials or refinements; a uniform estimate if a limit is involved; the classical conclusion only afterward; and an adversarial proof of the corresponding falsifier.

In Hodge, those data are the unitriangular table, the recurrence of $\sigma$, the
extension over the fiber product, its naturality, and the
Mittag--Leffler passage. In BSD, they are the root--FS--lattice partition, the complete
Kato--FERS table, the page and gluing maps of $\Psi_{\rm PT}$, the
Sha--Tamagawa cocycle maps, and the four based comparators. The equations cited in the preceding chapters display their mathematical localizers; auxiliary controls remain thereafter and do not reopen those closures.
