\section{Graded completion of the channels and the exclusion principle}
\label{vi:sec:fock-pauli}

The construction of a particle provides a space of channels, their labels,
and the operators that transport orientation and memory. The composition
of several particles adds a different question: how these channels are
exchanged and how their occupations are counted. Here this stratum is
constructed from the data already produced by APP--TRIT--TPK. Mass and the
Hamiltonian can subsequently act on the resulting space; their values
do not enter the proof of the occupation rules.

The parity used is that of sheet transport in the one-particle representation.
It is not the sign of charge, the parity of a visible integer, or the third
coordinate of the TRIT considered in isolation. The different readings
retain their maps. Section~\ref{vi:sec:conjugacion} constructs the oriented
return and spinorial representation; this section develops the
multiparticle algebra corresponding to a stated grading and exchange rule.

\subsection{Finite channel space and parity character}
\label{vi:subsec:canales-graduados}

At a fixed depth \(K\), let \(S_K\) be the finite set of
channels of the realization under consideration. Its elements retain the
route and representation data that still distinguish states:
sheet, orientation, boundary, memory, flavour, colour, and spin component
when they belong to the sector. The quotient defining \(S_K\)
is taken before constructing occupation; it does not identify two channels
solely because their masses are equal.

The one-particle space is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_K=\bigoplus_{s\in S_K}V_s,
 \qquad\langle e_{s,a},e_{t,b}\rangle=\delta_{st}\delta_{ab},
 \label{vi:eq:espacio-canales-fock}
\end{equation}
where \(V_s\) carries the stated representation multiplicity.
Each \(e_{s,a}\) is a complete mode, not an unlabelled atlas cell.
Its positive inner product belongs to that finite realization and makes
explicit the normalization used below.

The parity datum is a compositional character of transport,
\begin{equation}
 p:\operatorname{Mor}(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{TPK}})
 \longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
 \qquad p(\gamma\eta)=p(\gamma)+p(\eta).
 \label{vi:eq:caracter-paridad-fock}
\end{equation}
On the channels on which this grading is realized, the holonomy involution
\(J_p\) satisfies \(J_pe=(-1)^{p(e)}e\). Thus,
\[
 \mathcal H_K=\mathcal H_{K,\bar0}\oplus\mathcal H_{K,\bar1},
 \qquad \Pi_{\bar0}=\frac{I+J_p}{2},\quad
 \Pi_{\bar1}=\frac{I-J_p}{2}.
\]
The two projections are orthogonal because \(J_p^*=J_p\) and
\(J_p^2=I\). Equality \eqref{vi:eq:caracter-paridad-fock} ensures
that composing transports preserves the grading. When a realization has
not supplied this character, the completion below is a construction
relative to its explicit choice, not a statistical assignment obtained
from the appearance of a route.

\subsection{Graded exchange and permutation projections}
\label{vi:subsec:intercambio-graduado}

For homogeneous vectors, define the exchange
\begin{equation}
 \tau_p(v\otimes w)=(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}w\otimes v.
 \label{vi:eq:intercambio-graduado}
\end{equation}
The sign is negative exactly when both modes belong to the odd sector.
Exchange does not modify a mode's labels: it transports the complete
mode from one tensor position to another.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[Graded representation of permutations]
\label{vi:prop:permutaciones-graduadas}
The adjacent exchanges \(\tau_{p,j}\) on
\(\mathcal H_K^{\otimes n}\) are unitary and satisfy
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \tau_{p,j}^2=I,\qquad
 \tau_{p,j}\tau_{p,k}=\tau_{p,k}\tau_{p,j}\quad(|j-k|>1),\\
 \tau_{p,j}\tau_{p,j+1}\tau_{p,j}
 =\tau_{p,j+1}\tau_{p,j}\tau_{p,j+1}.
\end{gathered}
\]
They therefore define a unitary representation \(U_p\) of
\(\mathfrak S_n\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Applying an exchange twice gives the sign
\((-1)^{2p(v)p(w)}=1\) and restores both vectors.
Operators acting on disjoint pairs commute. On a homogeneous triple
of degrees \(a,b,c\), both sides of the three-exchange relation
produce the same final order and the sign
\((-1)^{ab+ac+bc}\). Each operator permutes an orthonormal basis
while multiplying it by signs of modulus one, and is therefore unitary.
\end{proof}

The average
\[
 P_n^p=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_n}U_p(\pi)
\]
is the orthogonal projection onto tensors invariant under graded exchange.
On a homogeneous even space it coincides with the ordinary symmetrizer;
on a homogeneous odd space it coincides with the antisymmetrizer.
If \(U\) denotes the unsigned permutation,
\begin{equation}
 S_n=\frac1{n!}\sum_\pi U(\pi),\qquad
 A_n=\frac1{n!}\sum_\pi\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)U(\pi).
 \label{vi:eq:simetrizadores-fock}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact symmetrization and antisymmetrization]
\label{vi:prop:proyectores-fock}
One has \(S_n^*=S_n^2=S_n\) and \(A_n^*=A_n^2=A_n\).
For \(n\ge2\), \(S_nA_n=0\). Their images are, respectively,
\(\operatorname{Sym}^n\mathcal H\) and \(\bigwedge^n\mathcal H\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Taking adjoints replaces each permutation by its inverse and leaves
the sums invariant. In the square of each average, every permutation
\(\rho\) occurs \(n!\) times; in the second average its coefficient
is \(\operatorname{sgn}(\rho)\). This proves idempotence.
The product of the two projections includes the sum of the signs
of all permutations, which is zero for \(n\ge2\).
Finally, their images are the invariant and alternating tensors,
by their averaging formulas.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Universal algebra and normalization of occupation states}
\label{vi:subsec:fock-construccion}

Let \(T(\mathcal H)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathcal H^{\otimes n}\)
be the tensor algebra. The graded algebraic completion is the quotient
by the ideal generated by
\begin{equation}
 v\otimes w-(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}w\otimes v.
 \label{vi:eq:ideal-fock}
\end{equation}
The universal property of the quotient means that every graded linear map
from the channels to an algebra satisfying these relations extends uniquely
to a homomorphism from the quotient. Once stated, the exchange rule thus
fixes the product of occupations without recourse to target energies.

\begin{theorem}[Symmetric and exterior decomposition]
\label{vi:thm:fock-graduado}
If \(d_0=\dim\mathcal H_{\bar0}\) and
\(d_1=\dim\mathcal H_{\bar1}\), the graded completion has
the decomposition
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F(\mathcal H)=
 \mathcal F_+(\mathcal H_{\bar0})\otimes
 \mathcal F_-(\mathcal H_{\bar1}),
 \label{vi:eq:fock-producto}
\end{equation}
\[
 \mathcal F_+(\mathcal H_{\bar0})=
 \bigoplus_{m\ge0}\operatorname{Sym}^m\mathcal H_{\bar0},
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_-(\mathcal H_{\bar1})=
 \bigoplus_{k=0}^{d_1}\bigwedge^k\mathcal H_{\bar1}.
\]
The total-number sector \(n\) is
\[
 \mathcal F_n(\mathcal H)=
 \bigoplus_{k=0}^{\min(n,d_1)}
 \operatorname{Sym}^{n-k}\mathcal H_{\bar0}
 \otimes\bigwedge^k\mathcal H_{\bar1}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
With ordered homogeneous bases fixed, relations
\eqref{vi:eq:ideal-fock} allow the even factors to be placed first
and the odd factors afterwards. Even factors commute; odd factors
anticommute, and the square of an odd factor is zero because we work
in characteristic zero. Each monomial is written as
\[
 e_1^{m_1}\cdots e_{d_0}^{m_{d_0}}
 f_{i_1}\cdots f_{i_k},
 \qquad m_j\ge0,\quad i_1<\cdots<i_k.
\]
The realization by symmetric and alternating tensors makes these
monomials linearly independent. It provides the inverse map and proves
the decomposition. Grouping them by
\(m_1+\cdots+m_{d_0}+k=n\) gives the final assertion.
\end{proof}

The Hilbert completion declares the normalized occupation vectors
\(|\boldsymbol m;I\rangle\) orthonormal, where
\(\boldsymbol m\in\mathbb N_0^{d_0}\) and
\(I\subset\{1,\ldots,d_1\}\). In the bosonic sector of degree
\(n=\sum_jm_j\), the tensor normalization is
\[
 |\boldsymbol m\rangle=
 \sqrt{\frac{n!}{\prod_jm_j!}}\,
 S_n(e_1^{\otimes m_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes
 e_{d_0}^{\otimes m_{d_0}}).
\]
For \(I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\}\), the exterior vector is
\[
 |I\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt{k!}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_k}
 \operatorname{sgn}(\pi)
 f_{i_{\pi(1)}}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{i_{\pi(k)}}.
\]
In the first formula there are \(n!/\prod m_j!\) distinct tensors;
in the second there are \(k!\). Counting them proves that both norms
are one. Degree zero is \(\mathbb C|0\rangle\); this occupation
vacuum is not identified with the constitutive chart of the electromagnetic
vacuum or with a vacancy in the TPK record.

\subsection{Fermionic creation and derivation of the CAR}
\label{vi:subsec:car-construccion}

Let \(I\) be an ordered subset of odd modes and
\(r_j(I)=\#\{i\in I:i<j\}\). Define
\begin{align}
 a_j^\dagger|I\rangle&=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&j\in I,\\
 (-1)^{r_j(I)}|I\cup\{j\}\rangle,&j\notin I,
 \end{cases}\notag\\
 a_j|I\rangle&=
 \begin{cases}
 (-1)^{r_j(I)}|I\setminus\{j\}\rangle,&j\in I,\\
 0,&j\notin I.
 \end{cases}
 \label{vi:eq:creacion-fermi}
\end{align}
The sign counts the transpositions needed to insert or remove the mode
while maintaining the order of the exterior basis. It uses neither its
mass, its charge, nor the sign of a expressed scalar value.
The matrices of the two maps are adjoints of one another and their norms
are \(1\), except in the trivial case with no odd modes.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical anticommutation relations]
\label{vi:thm:car-construidas}
The operators in \eqref{vi:eq:creacion-fermi} satisfy
\[
 \{a_i,a_j^\dagger\}=\delta_{ij}I,\qquad
 \{a_i,a_j\}=0,\qquad
 \{a_i^\dagger,a_j^\dagger\}=0.
\]
These are exact relations on the whole of
\(\mathcal F_-(\mathcal H_{\bar1})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(i=j\), the product \(a_j^\dagger a_j\) is the identity
on vectors containing \(j\) and zero on the others;
\(a_ja_j^\dagger\) has the complementary actions. Their sum
is \(I\). Two insertions or two removals of the same mode give zero.
For \(i\ne j\), if an operation is prohibited, both terms of
the corresponding anticommutator are zero. If both compositions are
admissible, they produce the same set of occupations.
Changing the order changes by one the number of modes preceding one
of the insertions or removals; the signs are opposite.
This proves the three anticommutators on every basis vector.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exclusion and occupation of a complete mode]
\label{vi:cor:pauli-ocupacion}
For \(N_j=a_j^\dagger a_j\),
\[
 N_j^*=N_j,\qquad N_j^2=N_j,\qquad
 \operatorname{Spec}(N_j)\subset\{0,1\}.
\]
The same complete mode admits at most one fermionic occupation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The CAR give
\[
 N_j^2=a_j^\dagger a_ja_j^\dagger a_j
 =a_j^\dagger(I-a_j^\dagger a_j)a_j=N_j,
\]
because \(a_j^2=(a_j^\dagger)^2=0\). Self-adjointness is immediate;
an eigenvalue of a self-adjoint idempotent is \(0\) or \(1\).
\end{proof}

The proof constructs the CAR before deducing Pauli exclusion: it does
not introduce exclusion as an additional restriction on a particle table.
It also explains the real generators used in
Proposition~\ref{vi:prop:car-real}: that proposition now acts on
the explicit exterior algebra. Its Majorana interpretation retains
the conditions of Section~\ref{vi:sec:topologia}.

\subsection{Bosonic creation, CCR, and the finite-particle domain}
\label{vi:subsec:ccr-construccion}

On the normalized bosonic basis, define
\begin{equation}
 b_j^\dagger|\boldsymbol m\rangle=
 \sqrt{m_j+1}\,|\boldsymbol m+\boldsymbol e_j\rangle,
 \qquad
 b_j|\boldsymbol m\rangle=
 \sqrt{m_j}\,|\boldsymbol m-\boldsymbol e_j\rangle,
 \label{vi:eq:creacion-bose}
\end{equation}
with value zero in the second expression when \(m_j=0\).
The factors arise from the normalization of symmetric tensors.
The common domain \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fin}}\) is the algebraic
direct sum of the number sectors: vectors have only finitely many
nonzero occupation components. It is dense and invariant under
the preceding maps.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical commutation relations]
\label{vi:thm:ccr-construidas}
On \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fin}}\),
\[
 [b_i,b_j^\dagger]=\delta_{ij}I,\qquad
 [b_i,b_j]=[b_i^\dagger,b_j^\dagger]=0.
\]
The number operators \(N_j^B=b_j^\dagger b_j\) satisfy
\(N_j^B|\boldsymbol m\rangle=m_j|\boldsymbol m\rangle\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the same mode,
\(b_jb_j^\dagger|\boldsymbol m\rangle=(m_j+1)|\boldsymbol m\rangle\)
and \(b_j^\dagger b_j|\boldsymbol m\rangle=m_j|\boldsymbol m\rangle\).
The difference is the identity. For distinct modes, the factors affect
different occupation coordinates and therefore commute.
The number formulas follow by applying the two operations in
\eqref{vi:eq:creacion-bose} consecutively.
\end{proof}

If there is an even mode, \(b_j\) and \(b_j^\dagger\) are unbounded
operators on the Hilbert completion: their norms on
\(|m_j\rangle\) grow as square roots of \(m_j\).
The preceding equalities therefore have an explicit domain and are
not presented as identities between finite matrices on the entire
bosonic space. The fermionic operators act on the second factor of
\eqref{vi:eq:fock-producto}; the bosonic operators act on the first.
On the common domain, operators on these two factors commute.

\begin{proposition}[Exact defect of a hard bosonic cutoff]
\label{vi:prop:corte-bosonico}
In one mode, let \(P_N\) be the projection onto
\(|0\rangle,\ldots,|N\rangle\) and let \(b^{(N)}=P_NbP_N\).
On this finite space,
\[
 [b^{(N)},(b^{(N)})^\dagger]
 =I-(N+1)|N\rangle\langle N|.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For \(m<N\), creation and annihilation retain the calculation
\((m+1)-m=1\). On \(|N\rangle\), truncated creation is zero
and the reverse product equals \(N\); the commutator equals
\(-N=1-(N+1)\). These are all the basis vectors.
\end{proof}

The correction resides at the last level of the cutoff, not in the
occupation law. A finite calculation can verify the CCR away from
that boundary and must retain the boundary term when it includes
the terminal level. This distinction is indispensable if a matrix
certificate is not to conflate a truncation with the full bosonic
completion.

\subsection{The meaning of “the same state” in the Pauli principle}
\label{vi:subsec:pauli-etiquetas-hojas}

Exclusion concerns the same vector of a complete mode. Two channels
may share a cell, mass, or reduced signature while remaining linearly
independent through their spin, sheet, colour, flavour, or another
coordinate preserved by transport. These two vectors admit a nonzero
exterior product. If a physical quotient identifies them, that quotient
must act before one states how many independent modes there are.

\begin{proposition}[Independence criterion for double occupation]
\label{vi:prop:pauli-gram}
For two vectors in a channel space,
\[
 \|u\wedge v\|^2
 =\|u\|^2\|v\|^2-|\langle u,v\rangle|^2.
\]
In particular, two orthonormal modes produce an exterior state of norm
one, and two proportional vectors produce zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The degree-two exterior realization is
\((u\otimes v-v\otimes u)/\sqrt2\). Expanding its norm gives
the formula. Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz characterizes linear
dependence; orthonormality gives squared norm one.
\end{proof}

For an orbital channel \(u\) and two orthogonal spin components
\(\xi_+,\xi_-\), the modes \(u\otimes\xi_+\) and
\(u\otimes\xi_-\) are distinct and their exterior product can be
occupied. There is no double occupation of the same mode: there is
occupation of two distinct components of the representation.
The same applies to two sheets when these remain independent components
of the realization.

The central electronic word may be fixed by \(C_M\), while the
charge representation distinguishes electron and positron.
This coincidence of words does not identify their complete modes or
determine their occupation. The word involution, cellular inversion,
and exchange of Fock factors retain the differentiated domains of
Section~\ref{vi:sec:conjugacion}.

If \(r\) orthonormal fermionic modes have the same value of a
partial observable, their total occupation can take the values
\(0,1,\ldots,r\). Each \(N_j\) remains a projection;
the sum \(\sum_{j=1}^rN_j\) need not be one.
This consequence distinguishes spectral degeneracy from exclusion
of a mode, two properties that an isolated mass value does not separate.

\subsection{Finite dimensions and transport by refinement}
\label{vi:subsec:fock-refinamiento}

\begin{proposition}[Occupation census]
\label{vi:prop:censo-fock}
For \(d\) modes,
\[
 \dim\bigwedge^n\mathbb C^d=\binom dn,
 \qquad
 \dim\operatorname{Sym}^n\mathbb C^d=\binom{d+n-1}{n}
 \quad(d\ge1).
\]
The first dimension is zero for \(n>d\). In the graded space,
the dimensions for total number \(n\) are obtained by summing
the products of the two dimensions for
\(k=0,\ldots,\min(n,d_1)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An exterior basis is chosen through subsets of \(n\) distinct modes.
A symmetric basis is chosen through
\((m_1,\ldots,m_d)\in\mathbb N_0^d\) with sum \(n\).
Placing \(d-1\) separators among \(n\) occupations counts
these weak compositions and gives the second binomial coefficient.
The graded formula follows from \eqref{vi:eq:fock-producto} by
direct sums and tensor products.
\end{proof}

Let \(T:\mathcal H_K\to\mathcal H_L\) be the stated refinement
intertwiners, preserving the grading and corresponding channel data.
Each induces
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma(T)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}T^{\otimes n}|_{\mathcal F_n},
 \qquad T^{\otimes0}=I_{\mathbb C}.
 \label{vi:eq:segunda-cuantizacion-transporte}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Compatibility of completion with transport]
\label{vi:thm:fock-naturalidad}
If \(T\) preserves degree, then
\(T^{\otimes n}P_n^p=P_n^{p'}T^{\otimes n}\).
If, in addition, \(\|T\|\le1\), then \(\Gamma(T)\) is a
bounded operator of norm at most \(1\) on Fock space and
\[
 \Gamma(T_2T_1)=\Gamma(T_2)\Gamma(T_1),\qquad
 \Gamma(I)=I.
\]
Consequently, compatible refinement squares for the channels
induce compatible occupation squares.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Preservation of degree makes \(T^{\otimes n}\) commute with
each adjacent exchange; by
Proposition~\ref{vi:prop:permutaciones-graduadas}, it commutes
with the permutations and their average. The restrictions are
therefore well defined. Their norm is bounded by
\(\|T\|^n\le1\), so the direct sum is bounded.
Identities and compositions are verified in each degree and,
by continuity, on the completion.
\end{proof}

Contractivity is a sufficient condition for treating the bosonic sum
without additional restrictions. In finite degrees, the powers exist
for any linear map, but their sum is not always bounded:
for one bosonic mode and \(T=2I\),
\(\Gamma(T)|n\rangle=2^n|n\rangle\).
In the finite exterior sector there are only \(d_1+1\) degrees,
so any linear map of that sector has a bounded finite second
quantization. The difference concerns domains, not causality.

\begin{proposition}[Transport of creations and annihilations]
\label{vi:prop:fock-transporte-adjunto}
Let \(T:\mathcal H_K\to\mathcal H_L\) be a bounded
degree-preserving transport. For \(f\in\mathcal H_K\) and
\(g\in\mathcal H_L\), on the algebraic finite-particle domain
one has
\begin{align}
 \Gamma(T)c_K^\dagger(f)
 &=c_L^\dagger(Tf)\Gamma(T),
 \label{vi:eq:fock-transporte-creacion}\\
 c_L(g)\Gamma(T)
 &=\Gamma(T)c_K(T^*g).
 \label{vi:eq:fock-transporte-aniquilacion}
\end{align}
The subscripts indicate the fibre on which each operator acts.
If \(T\) is isometric, the second identity specializes to
\[
 c_L(Tf)\Gamma(T)=\Gamma(T)c_K(f),
 \qquad
 N_{L,Tf}\Gamma(T)=\Gamma(T)N_{K,f},
\]
where \(N_{K,f}=c_K^\dagger(f)c_K(f)\) and, for a unit mode,
\(N_{L,Tf}\) is its transported occupation operator.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In a symmetric or exterior product, applying \(T\) to every factor
commutes with inserting \(f\), which becomes \(Tf\).
This proves \eqref{vi:eq:fock-transporte-creacion}.
Annihilation contracts each factor with the observed mode.
For each factor \(v\),
\[
 \langle g,Tv\rangle=\langle T^*g,v\rangle.
\]
The exterior or graded signs are preserved because \(T\)
preserves degree; the symmetric normalizations are the same on
both sides. The sum of contractions proves
\eqref{vi:eq:fock-transporte-aniquilacion}. If \(T^*T=I\),
substitute \(g=Tf\). Composing the creation and annihilation
identities then gives the occupation identity.
All operations considered preserve the finite-particle domain;
when \(\|T\|\le1\), \(\Gamma(T)\) also has the bounded extension
of Theorem~\ref{vi:thm:fock-naturalidad}.
\end{proof}

The general identity retains the adjoint \(T^*\) and is valid without
isometry. The need for the isometric condition arises when transporting
the same mode. For example, if \(T=tI\), with \(0<t<1\),
for a unit mode \(f\) and the vacuum \(\Omega\),
\[
 c(tf)\Gamma(T)f=t^2\Omega,
 \qquad \Gamma(T)c(f)f=\Omega.
\]
The general formula correctly gives
\(\Gamma(T)c(T^*tf)f=t^2\Omega\). Transport by contractions,
with its explicit adjoint, and the isometric specialization of occupation
are thereby retained.

\subsection{Exchange symmetry and the record of a composite}
\label{vi:subsec:pauli-composicion}

The composition in Section~\ref{vi:subsec:composicion-registro}
acts first on histories and their boundaries. Fock space organizes
channel occupation; \(\mathfrak C_{12}\) organizes the linkage
record. These are coordinated operations that do not replace each
other. In particular, antisymmetrization does not remove the
memory of the coupling order.

Let \(\mathcal D\subset\mathcal H^{\otimes n}\) be the space
generated by constituent expressions admissible for an interface.
The linearization of composition is written
\(C_{\mathcal T}:\mathcal D\to\mathcal H_{\mathrm{comp}}\).
When a finite group \(G\) of exchanges preserves admissibility
and boundary, the specific compatibility is
\[
 C_{\mathcal T}U(g)=V(g)C_{\mathcal T},\qquad g\in G,
\]
where \(U,V\) are unitary representations and \(V\) transports
the composite records, including their sheet and memory data.
The equation is the exchange condition for that realization;
it is not inferred from the mere census of expressions.

\begin{proposition}[Descent of composition to the statistical sector]
\label{vi:prop:composicion-estadistica}
Let \(\chi:G\to\{+1,-1\}\) be the character of the exchange
sector. Under the preceding compatibility,
\[
 P_\chi^{\mathrm{comp}}C_{\mathcal T}
 =C_{\mathcal T}P_\chi^{\mathrm{in}},
\]
where
\[
 P_\chi^{\mathrm{in}}=\frac1{|G|}\sum_g\chi(g)U(g),
 \qquad
 P_\chi^{\mathrm{comp}}=\frac1{|G|}\sum_g\chi(g)V(g).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Multiplying each intertwining equality by
\(\chi(g)/|G|\) and summing gives exactly the identity.
The averages are projections by the same calculation as in
Proposition~\ref{vi:prop:proyectores-fock}.
\end{proof}

The result justifies composing within a symmetric or alternating
sector when the boundary has the required transport.
If a permutation changes the physical linkage channel, the distinct
trees and their interrelations are retained; they are not identified
by a symmetrization that forgets that boundary.

\subsubsection{Colour antisymmetry and the remainder of the baryonic state}

In the baryonic realization,
Proposition~\ref{vi:prop:singlete-barion} constructs the colour
vector \(|\varepsilon\rangle\), which changes sign under a
transposition. After regrouping the factors of the complete state,
a factorized vector has the form
\[
 \Psi=|\varepsilon\rangle_{\mathrm{col}}
 \otimes\Phi_{\mathrm{spin,flavour,orbital,sheet}}.
\]
Exchange of two constituents acts simultaneously on both factors.
If \(U_{\mathrm{rest}}(\pi)\Phi=\Phi\), then the complete state
changes sign and belongs to the fermionic sector.
Conversely, for a factorized vector with a nonzero colour singlet,
total alternation requires the remainder to be symmetric under
those same permutations. The proof consists in factoring
\(U_{\mathrm{tot}}(\pi)=U_{\mathrm{col}}(\pi)\otimes
U_{\mathrm{rest}}(\pi)\) and cancelling the colour sign.

The word “remainder” explicitly includes flavour and sheet.
Exchanging colours without transporting those components is not
the permutation of two complete modes. For a nonfactorizable state,
the alternating projection is applied to the complete tensor space,
without replacing it by a scalar rule.

\subsubsection{Compatibility of physical projections}

Colour, channel, and statistics projections can be combined by
multiplication when they commute. If \(P,Q\) are orthogonal
projections and \([P,Q]=0\), then
\((PQ)^*=PQ\), \((PQ)^2=PQ\), and
\(\operatorname{im}(PQ)=\operatorname{im}P\cap\operatorname{im}Q\).
Inclusion in both images is immediate; for a vector in their
intersection, \(PQv=v\).
If they do not commute, their product does not generally represent
the projection for the two simultaneous conditions: the realization
must construct the common subspace or the physical order of the
operations.

This precision preserves the meaning of the singlet and Pauli
projections that occur in the composites of
Section~\ref{vi:sec:familias-compuestos}. Coupling selects a
sector; the composite mass is evaluated after constructing
that sector's linkage record.

\subsection{Action of the material operator and scope of physical identification}
\label{vi:subsec:fock-alcance}

Let \(B=B^*\) be a channel operator already obtained from internal
dynamics and preserving the grading. In degree \(n\) it acts through
\[
 d\Gamma_n(B)=\sum_{j=1}^n
 I^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes B\otimes I^{\otimes(n-j)}.
\]
The sum commutes with graded permutations and restricts to
\(\mathcal F_n\). If \(B\ge0\), every restriction is positive.
In an eigenbasis, its value is the sum of occupations multiplied
by the eigenvalues of \(B\), with the occupations allowed by the
preceding proofs. This is the action of an additive observable on
independent channels; it does not identify the operator of a bound
composite with the sum of its constituents' isolated masses.

In the bosonic Hilbert sum, \(d\Gamma(B)\) has the natural domain
\[
 \left\{\psi=(\psi_n):
 \sum_n\|d\Gamma_n(B)\psi_n\|^2<\infty\right\}.
\]
Each block is self-adjoint in finite dimension, and its direct sum
with this domain is self-adjoint. The linkage or open-channel
operator is added only when it has been constructed by the corresponding
dynamics. The formation of a complex pole follows the treatment in
\ref{vi:subsec:resonancias-familias}.

Complete algebraic results have therefore been obtained:
symmetry projections, normalized bases, CAR and CCR on their domains,
single-mode exclusion, occupation censuses, and compatibility with
refinement intertwiners. The starting point of these results is
stated in \eqref{vi:eq:espacio-canales-fock},
\eqref{vi:eq:caracter-paridad-fock}, and
\eqref{vi:eq:intercambio-graduado}.

When the realization of rotations satisfies
\(U(2\pi)=(-1)^p\), the same grading coordinates the restoration
of orientation at \(4\pi\) and the exterior completion.
That compatibility is not replaced by a coincidence between two
signs. Identification with the relativistic spin--statistics theorem
additionally requires the Lorentzian representation, locality,
and positivity of the physical realization. Braiding sectors that
do not descend to permutations remain in the codomain of
Section~\ref{vi:sec:topologia}; they are not forced into this
bosonic or fermionic completion.
