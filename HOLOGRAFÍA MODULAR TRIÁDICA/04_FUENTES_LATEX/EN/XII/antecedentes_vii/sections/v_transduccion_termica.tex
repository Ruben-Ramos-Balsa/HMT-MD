\section{Genealogical memory, informational reduction, and thermal transduction}
\label{v:ant:sec:ii-kb-aut-desarrollo}

The thermal construction uses two readouts of the same
APP--TRIT--TPK development. The first preserves the states, admissible paths,
and record information; the second supplies the oriented action
and the return duration. Their composition determines an energy per
tritic decision. Temperature and energy coordinates are introduced
subsequently through explicit dimensional maps. This sequence
identifies which information is preserved, which information is read out
by a reader, and which quantity converts that information into dimensional entropy.

\subsection{Reader, memory, and state recovery}

Fix a finite level of the development and let \(\Omega\) denote the
set of admissible enriched states. The record preserves the sheet,
orientation, remainder, quotient, carry, path, phase, and memory on the
domains where each coordinate enters. Let
\[
 q:\Omega\longrightarrow Y,\qquad
 M:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathcal M
\]
be the visible readout and the retained memory, respectively. For a
distribution \(p\) on \(\Omega\), write
\(\mathsf H(p)=-\sum_xp_x\log p_x\), with \(0\log0=0\).
The logarithm is applied to dimensionless probabilities.

\begin{definition}[Genealogical recovery of a readout]
The pair \(\widehat q=(q,M)\) is a recoverable reader on the support
\(\Omega_p\) when there exists
\[
 d:\widehat q(\Omega_p)\longrightarrow\Omega_p,
 \qquad d\circ\widehat q=\operatorname{id}_{\Omega_p}.
\]
The fibre information of \(q\) is
\(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{fib}}(q;p)=\mathsf H(X\mid q(X))\), where
\(X\) has law \(p\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Recovery identity and cost of reduction]
For a recoverable reader,
\begin{align}
 \mathsf H(X\mid q(X),M(X))&=0,\\
 \mathsf H(X)&=\mathsf H(q(X))+\mathsf H(X\mid q(X)),\\
 \mathsf H(X\mid q(X))&=\mathsf H(M(X)\mid q(X)).
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-fibra}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The map \(d\) recovers \(X\) from the pair \((q(X),M(X))\),
so the first conditional entropy vanishes. Since \(q\) is a
function of \(X\), the chain rule gives the second equality.
For the third, expand
\(\mathsf H(X,M(X)\mid q(X))\) in two ways. One yields
\(\mathsf H(X\mid q(X))\), because \(M\) is a function of \(X\);
the other yields
\(\mathsf H(M(X)\mid q(X))+\mathsf H(X\mid q(X),M(X))\).
\end{proof}

Euclidean division on the APP sheets exhibits the elementary
arithmetic mechanism. For \(i,j\in\{1,\ldots,9\}\), one preserves
\[
 i+j=9q^+_{ij}+R^+_{ij},\qquad
 ij=9q^\times_{ij}+R^\times_{ij},\qquad
 1\leq R^\sigma_{ij}\leq9\quad(\sigma\in\{+,\times\}).
\]
The remainder--quotient pair recovers the integer value of the operation.
Summing the differences over the eighty-one positions gives
\[
 2025-810=54+9\cdot129.
\]
The quotient memory restores the contribution not read out by the
residual reduction. Recoverability of the complete state additionally
requires preservation of the relevant position and path coordinates;
the remainder--quotient pair here identifies the result of the operation.

\subsection{Reversible updating, tritic decision, and erasure}

Let \(U:\Omega\to\Omega\) be the TPK update on a domain where
the enriched transport has an inverse. The transported law is
\(p'_y=p_{U^{-1}y}\). The bijection preserves the multiset of
probabilities and therefore
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf H(U(X))=\mathsf H(X),\qquad
 \mathsf H(X\mid U(X))=0.
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-reversible}
\end{equation}
If the observed output is \(q(U(X))\), the information not read out
is quantified by \(\mathsf H(X\mid q(U(X)))\). The complete state,
its readout, and the resetting of a record thus define three distinct maps.

For the three oriented states of the TRIT, let
\(p=(p_{-1},p_0,p_{+1})\) be a normalized distribution. Its information is
\begin{equation}
 I_{\mathrm{TRIT}}(p)=-\sum_{a=-1}^{1}p_a\log p_a,
 \qquad 0\leq I_{\mathrm{TRIT}}(p)\leq\log3.
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-trit}
\end{equation}
The upper endpoint corresponds to \(p_a=1/3\). Indeed,
\[
 D(p\Vert(1/3,1/3,1/3))
 =\sum_ap_a\log(3p_a)=\log3-I_{\mathrm{TRIT}}(p)\geq0;
\]
the inequality follows from the convexity of \(t\log t\), and
equality characterizes the uniform distribution. The lower endpoint
corresponds to a distribution concentrated on one state.

The ideal Landauer realization uses a memory with energetically
degenerate logical states, an isothermal environment at temperature \(\Theta\),
and a balance that preserves the output of the logical map and accounts for
the auxiliary records. In this model, the decrease in logical entropy is
\(k_B[\mathsf H(X)-\mathsf H(F(X))]
=k_B\mathsf H(X\mid F(X))\) for a deterministic map \(F\).
The entropy balance requires at least this amount to be transferred to the environment;
its energetic expression is \(Q\geq k_B\Theta\mathsf H(X\mid F(X))\).
The distinction between reversible logical evolution and physical erasure
corresponds to the Landauer--Bennett realization \cite{bennett2003}.
The invertible update
\eqref{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-reversible} has zero logical contribution; resetting
a uniform triple requires the transfer of \(\log3\) nats
to the environment and satisfies \(Q\geq k_B\Theta\log3\).
The control energy and dissipation of a specific physical realization
belong to its additional thermodynamic balance.

\subsection{Action, frequency, and decision energy}

Let \(\mathcal L_{\rm act}\) and \(\mathcal L_T\) be the
dimensional lines of action and duration; the energy line is
\(\mathcal L_E=\mathcal L_{\rm act}\otimes\mathcal L_T^*\).
The preceding development supplies the positive sections
\(\hbar_a\in\mathcal L_{\rm act}\), with
\(a\in\{\mathrm{pre},\mathrm{ret}\}\), and the elementary duration
\(t_0\in\mathcal L_T\). The complete calendar supplies
\[
 T_{108}=108t_0,\qquad
 \omega_{108}=\frac{2\pi}{T_{108}},\qquad
 h_a=2\pi\hbar_a.
\]
The dimensional contraction between action and frequency defines
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_a:=\hbar_a\omega_{108}
 =\frac{h_a}{108t_0}
 =\frac{\hbar_a}{t_P},\qquad
 t_P=\frac{54}{\pi}t_0.
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-energia}
\end{equation}
The final expression uses the same reduced circular period as the
gravitational section. The three expressions correspond to a single
energy section, with the orientation \(a\) retained in every
factor. The normalized information of a uniform decision
yields the energy section per nat
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_a:=\frac{\epsilon_a}{\log3}\in\mathcal L_E^+.
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-energia-nat}
\end{equation}
The previously constructed action determines the energy scale once
the duration is fixed; the cardinality of the TRIT alternatives determines its
informational normalization. The value \(\log3\) enters through the
counting and measure specified in \eqref{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-trit}.

\subsection{Dimensional definition of Boltzmann's constant and coordinate selection}

Let \(\mathcal L_\Theta\) be the oriented temperature line.
The dimensional entropy space is
\(\mathcal L_{\rm ent}=\mathcal L_E\otimes\mathcal L_\Theta^*\).
A positive linear map
\(k_B:\mathcal L_\Theta\to\mathcal L_E\) is equivalently
a positive element of \(\mathcal L_{\rm ent}\). The same
object can therefore act on a temperature or multiply dimensionless
information.

\begin{definition}[Thermal normalization of the tritic decision]
A thermal pair compatible with the oriented section \(a\) consists of
a positive transducer \(k_B\) and a positive section
\(\Theta_{{\rm clk},a}\in\mathcal L_\Theta\) such that
\begin{equation}
 k_B(\Theta_{{\rm clk},a})=\varepsilon_a,
 \qquad
 k_B\Theta_{{\rm clk},a}\log3=\epsilon_a.
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-par-termico}
\end{equation}
Boltzmann's constant serves as the energy--temperature
transducer of tritic information in this realization.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness relative to the thermal section and dimensional covariance]
For a fixed positive section \(\Theta_{{\rm clk},a}\), there exists a
unique positive linear map satisfying
\eqref{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-par-termico}. For positive bases \(u_E,u_\Theta\),
if
\[
 \epsilon_a=\epsilon_a^{\rm num}u_E,\quad
 \Theta_{{\rm clk},a}=\theta_a u_\Theta,\quad
 k_B(u_\Theta)=b\,u_E,
\]
its coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 b=\frac{\epsilon_a^{\rm num}}{\theta_a\log3}.
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-coordenada}
\end{equation}
Under \(u'_E=s_Eu_E\), \(u'_\Theta=s_\Theta u_\Theta\), with
\(s_E,s_\Theta>0\), one has
\[
 b'=\frac{s_\Theta}{s_E}b,\qquad
 \theta'_a=\frac{\theta_a}{s_\Theta},\qquad
 (\epsilon_a^{\rm num})'=\frac{\epsilon_a^{\rm num}}{s_E}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A nonzero section spans a line. Specifying its image determines
a unique linear map; positivity of both sections proves
positivity of the map. Substitution of the coordinates into
\eqref{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-par-termico} gives
\(b\theta_a\log3=\epsilon_a^{\rm num}\). The three transformation
laws follow by expressing the same sections and map
in the new bases.
\end{proof}

The same Boltzmann constant must act in both orientations.
Since \(t_P\) is common, simultaneous compatibility is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Theta_{{\rm clk},{\rm pre}}}{\Theta_{{\rm clk},{\rm ret}}}
 =\frac{\epsilon_{\rm pre}}{\epsilon_{\rm ret}}
 =\frac{\hbar_{\rm pre}}{\hbar_{\rm ret}}.
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-orientaciones}
\end{equation}
Indeed, an isomorphism of lines preserves ratios of sections.
Conversely, if the preceding equality holds, the isomorphism
determined in one orientation also satisfies the normalization in
the other. Thus, the return changes the thermal section of the cycle
in the same proportion as the energy, while the transducer remains
common to both readouts.

The preceding equation makes explicit the choice required to read out an
individual value. If the thermal section has not yet been selected
by an independent reader, then, for every \(\lambda>0\), the pair
\[
 (k_B,\Theta_{{\rm clk},a})
 \longmapsto(\lambda k_B,\lambda^{-1}\Theta_{{\rm clk},a})
\]
preserves \(\epsilon_a\). Consequently, presenting an
internally selected individual coordinate requires specification of the reader for
\(\Theta_{{\rm clk},a}\), the bases, and their dimensional transport.
The action and information equations remain determined before
this specification. The subsequent identification of the bases with
the joule and kelvin belongs to the metrological readout of the result.

\subsection{Dimensional entropy and information of the reduced state}

For a distribution \(p\) of admissible paths, or a reduced state
with density matrix \(\rho\), let
\[
 s(p)=-\sum_xp_x\log p_x,\qquad
 s(\rho)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\log\rho).
\]
Their dimensional readouts are
\begin{equation}
 S(p)=k_Bs(p),\qquad S(\rho)=k_Bs(\rho)
 \quad\text{in }\mathcal L_{\rm ent}.
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-entropia}
\end{equation}
Evaluation at \(\Theta\) yields an energy
\(\Theta S=k_B\Theta\,s\). For the uniform triple at the cycle
temperature, \eqref{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-par-termico} gives exactly
\[
 S_{\rm TRIT}=k_B\log3,\qquad
 \Theta_{{\rm clk},a}S_{\rm TRIT}=\epsilon_a.
\]
If \(\rho_A\) is the reduction of the bipartite purification
constructed in the area section, \(s(\rho_A)\) measures the entanglement
of that bipartition. Unit transport preserves its
eigenvalues, orientation, and sector occupations. The area scale
\(\gamma a_0\ell_P^2\) converts occupations into area; the
transducer \(k_B\) converts information into dimensional entropy.
Both readouts use the same record through distinct operators.

\subsection{Spectral normalization of thermal histories}

Let \(\mathcal G\) be an already constructed finite graph of admissible extensions,
with adjacency matrix \(A\). Each edge \(e\) preserves
its path coordinates and receives a dimensionless action increment
\(a_*(e)>0\) from the corresponding reader. Once the energy section
\(\epsilon_*\) has been chosen, set \(t=\beta\epsilon_*\), where
\(\beta=(k_B\Theta)^{-1}\). The thermal operator is
\begin{equation}
 (L_tf)(y)=\sum_{e:x\to y}\exp[-t a_*(e)]f(x).
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-operador}
\end{equation}
All its exponents are dimensionless. The condition
\(\rho(L_t)=1\) normalizes the weighted growth of histories.

\begin{proposition}[Existence and uniqueness of normalized pressure]
Suppose that \(A\) is irreducible, \(\rho(A)>1\), and
\(0<m\leq a_*(e)\leq M\) for every edge. There exists a unique
\(t_*>0\) such that \(\rho(L_{t_*})=1\), and
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\log\rho(A)}{M}\leq t_*\leq
 \frac{\log\rho(A)}{m}.
 \label{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-presion}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Entrywise comparison yields
\(e^{-tM}A\leq L_t\leq e^{-tm}A\) for \(t\geq0\).
Monotonicity of the spectral radius of nonnegative matrices implies
the corresponding bounds for \(\rho(L_t)\). The spectral radius
is continuous, equals \(\rho(A)>1\) at zero, and tends to zero at infinity.
Moreover, for \(s>0\),
\(L_{t+s}\leq e^{-sm}L_t\), so that
\(\rho(L_{t+s})\leq e^{-sm}\rho(L_t)<\rho(L_t)\).
Continuity and strict decrease prove existence and
uniqueness. Substituting the value one into the initial bounds proves
\eqref{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-presion}.
\end{proof}

The local realization of three equally weighted alternatives, represented
by a vertex with three loops and \(a_*(e)=1\), satisfies
\(\rho(L_t)=3e^{-t}\). Its normalization gives \(t_*=\log3\).
With \(\epsilon_*=\epsilon_a\), one obtains
\(\beta_{\rm clk}=\log3/\epsilon_a\), exactly the relation
\eqref{v:ant:eq:ii-kb-aut-par-termico}. This calculation identifies the
local tritic coincidence. For a history graph with additional constraints,
coincidence with the same clock requires verification of
\[
 \rho\!\left(L_{(\log3/\epsilon_a)\epsilon_*}\right)=1
\]
with its effective increments. Local normalization and the pressure
of the complete system thus retain their respective domains.

The resulting relation brings together structure, value, and meaning. The record
determines recoverable information; the probabilities on the
continuations determine its entropic readout; action and period
determine \(\epsilon_a\); the energy--temperature transducer
reads out dimensional entropy. Area and entanglement are
related through the reduced state and its boundary operators,
while keeping explicit which datum produces each quantity.
