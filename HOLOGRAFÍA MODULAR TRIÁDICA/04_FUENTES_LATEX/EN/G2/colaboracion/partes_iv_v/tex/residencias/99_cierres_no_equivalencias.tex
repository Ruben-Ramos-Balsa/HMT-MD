\subsection{Obstructions of canonicity at the boundary
\texorpdfstring{\(108{:}144=90{:}120\)}{108:144=90:120}}

\begin{theorem}[There is no canonical inclusion
\texorpdfstring{\(6\hookrightarrow8\)}{6 to 8} without gauge]
\label{thm:hmt-final-no-inclusion-canonica-seis-ocho}
Let \(A\) and \(B\) be bare sets with

\[
 |A|=6,
 \qquad |B|=8.
\]
There is no rule, natural with respect to all relabelings of \(A\)
and \(B\), that selects an injection \(A\hookrightarrow B\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If an injection \(\iota:A\hookrightarrow B\) were selected without additional
data, naturality with respect to every \(\beta\in\operatorname{Sym}(B)\)
would force \(\beta\iota=\iota\). Given \(a\in A\), there exists a permutation
\(\beta\) that moves \(\iota(a)\), a contradiction. Therefore, the route-by-route
APP--Witt transducer is not canonical from the cardinalities. Once
an octad, an inclusion, and the incidence gauge are fixed, a calibrated
transducer is obtained; the choice forms part of its domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A bare fibre of cardinality \(28\) does not determine
\(J(8,2)\)]
\label{thm:hmt-final-no-johnson-desde-cardinal}
Let \(F\) be an unstructured set with \(|F|=28\). There is no nontrivial
adjacency relation on \(F\), natural with respect to all its
permutations, that produces the Johnson graph \(J(8,2)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The group \(\operatorname{Sym}(F)\) acts transitively on the unordered pairs
of distinct elements. A simple relation invariant under that entire group
therefore contains either all pairs or none. The two resulting graphs are
the complete graph and the empty graph. By contrast, \(J(8,2)\) has \(28\) vertices and
degree \(12\); it is neither of them. Consequently, the calibrated projection
\(1008\to36\) with uniform fibers of cardinality \(28\) does not by
itself imply a Witt octad. Once an octad \(O\) and a bijection
\(F\simeq\binom{O}{2}\) have been fixed, Johnson adjacency can be transported to \(F\),
but it depends precisely on that gauge.
\end{proof}

These two theorems close, by exclusion, the former canonical
formulations. They do not affect the exact invariant
\[
 \frac{108}{144}=\frac{90}{120}=\frac68
\]
nor to the normalized defect \(-1/12\), which belong to the rank extension
and not to the choice of a Witt incidence.

\subsection{Infinitely divisible channel induced by the TPK transfer}

\begin{theorem}[Poissonization UCP and coherent roots]
\label{thm:hmt-final-poissonizacion-ucp}
Let \(K\) be a UCP channel compatible with the TPK truncations\@. For \(t\ge0\),
define
\[
 P_t:=\exp\bigl(t(K-I)\bigr)
 =e^{-t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^n}{n!}K^n.
\]
Then \((P_t)_{t\ge0}\) is a UCP semigroup compatible with all
truncations, and
\[
 P_1=(P_{1/m})^m
 \qquad(m\ge1).
\]
At every finite level, a minimal Stinespring dilation of \(P_{1/m}\)
produces a coherent tensor root; the inverse limit preserves the
TPK marginals.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each \(P_t\) is a norm-convergent convex combination of the channels
\(K^n\), and is therefore UCP\@. Since all terms are powers of the same
operator, the exponential identity gives \(P_tP_s=P_{t+s}\). Compatibility
with truncations is verified term by term. Taking \(t=1/m\) yields
the \(m\)-th root. Stinespring converts each finite composition into
a tensor contraction; naturality of the channels makes their
marginals coherent.
\end{proof}

The theorem closes the existence of an infinitely divisible channel with
TPK memory\@. It does not assert that an arbitrary historical channel coincides with this
Poissonization: that equality would require comparing their generators and is
rejected unless such a comparison is provided.

Without those data, ambient equality is not left indeterminate: it is a stronger assertion and is not equivalent to the specific class already constructed in the subspace.
