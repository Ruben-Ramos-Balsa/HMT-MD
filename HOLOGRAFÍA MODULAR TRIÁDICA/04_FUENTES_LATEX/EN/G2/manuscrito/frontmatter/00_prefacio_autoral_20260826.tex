\section*{Complex interaction between two simple machines}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Complex interaction between two simple machines}

This document compiles the extensive technical material---for future publications---concerning the results obtained and the mathematical-physical developments carried out between 2023 and 2026 through the use of Large Language Model (LLM) tools. From this process arose what we designate, respectively, Triadic Modular Holography and Dimensional Mechanics (HMT–MD). The origin of this project, however, dates back to the beginning of the twenty-first century, specifically to the study of the discovery underlying the artifact invented by Oumar Haidara Fall in 2001. It should be noted here that this artifact was brought to the attention of the various competent academic and civil authorities in the respective places of origin of both authors.

The accumulated material and the knowledge progressively acquired over more than two decades of remark, study, and analysis, concerning both the artifact and its phenomenology, constituted the operational and informational basis that we subsequently applied to artificial-intelligence models.

Therefore, the HMT–MD result presented here, together with its derivations and proofs, is preceded by an empirical genealogy that, \textit{a priori}, might at best appear counterintuitive to the technical specialist—for it contravenes the postulates and predictions of the theoretical-interpretive framework in which the artifact is inscribed by historical tradition, namely, classical mechanics. Nevertheless, its metrological verification is decisive. Furthermore, once cognitive prejudices or confirmation biases are set aside, it becomes directly demonstrable by the facts.

Finally, one might consider something that remains surprising as a historical paradox: when we began reasoning with “thinking machines” in “the age of artificial intelligence,” we also had the serendipity to reveal an interaction unprecedented in ordinary mechanics and were thereby compelled to reconsider the relation between two simple machines: the lever and the wheel.
