\subsection{Action of the readers and content of the moments}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-5}

The explicit actions of the odometer, the difference reader, the uniform inclusion, the Helmert refinement, the bidirectional boundary and the polar chart have been recovered and composed. The calculation yields:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The moments of the positive and negative columns, considered separately, are determined by explicit operators. They include all cross products between tests.
\item The uniform expectation does not coincide with the Helmert refinement. On the carry mode it gives an exact factor, calculated below; it does not by itself convert a translation difference into the uniform mode.
\item The gamma-tail resolvent does perform the analytic conversion and exactly reconstructs all negative rows, over every window and before using a sign.
\item The simultaneous identification of the encoder's first moment and the moment of its projection is not obtained from the three preceding chart identities by a simple direct sum. The specific additional composition that must be verified is identified below. The structural reader is not declared absent, nor is this focal check identified with the full TPK.

\end{enumerate}
The advance is a calculation of action and cross products, not a reiteration that the projection is positive.

\subsection{Conventions and genealogical snapshot}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-32}

The relevant starting point is the APP--TRIT--TPK state, its refinement with memory and its native readers. In this formalization:

\begin{itemize}
\item APP provides the support and the selector of irreducibles. The native product, coproductivity and the selector precede the publication of prime--power labels.
\item TRIT and sheet transport preserve orientation and seam.
\item The TPK preserves the phase step and the carry record; its odometer induces the translation difference. The logarithmic publication of the clocks produces \(\ell_{p,k}=k\log p\) and \(w_{p,k}=(\log p)p^{-k/2}\).
\item The boundary and polar charts are subsequent realizations of the same readers. No state is selected by means of zeta zeros or a target value.

\end{itemize}
This section calculates the arrow between those readers and the expectation, after the common generation; it does not seek to reconstruct or replace the joint continuum.

We work with orthonormal history coordinates. In them \(u_N=N^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)\). If functions with uniform probability measure are used, the constant coordinates and averages are conjugated by the corresponding normalization factor; the projections and all the following norm ratios are the same.

Let \(H=L^2(\mathbb R)\), \(T_af(x)=f(x-a)\), \(\mathcal D_R=C_c^\infty((-R/2,R/2))\). We write \(d_a=T_a-I\); simultaneously changing to \(I-T_a\) preserves their Grams, but changes the sign of the inverting cotrace.

The inner product is linear in the first variable. The polar
evaluations and the scalar of the complete form are retained,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_f&=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{x/2}\,dx,
&B_f&=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-x/2}\,dx,\\
c_\Gamma&=-\gamma-\frac\pi2-3\log2-\log\pi<0,
&\mu(a)&=\frac{e^{-a/2}}{1-e^{-2a}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{viii:gamma-add-def-evaluaciones}
\end{equation}
Here \(\pi\), \(\gamma\) and the logarithms preserve the internal
publication and subsequent realization declared in the preceding chapters.
The formula does not introduce target values into the generator.

\subsection{Action of the odometer on generators}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-47}

For \(N=9^m\), \(m\ge1\), and the seam located at the first vector,

\begin{equation}
U_{a,N}(e_r\otimes f)=
\begin{cases}
e_{r+1}\otimes f,&r<N-1,\\
e_0\otimes T_af,&r=N-1.
\end{cases}
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-1}
\end{equation}

The uniformization \(i_Nf=u_N\otimes f\) gives

\begin{equation}
U_{a,N}i_Nf
=u_N\otimes f+N^{-1/2}e_0\otimes d_af.
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-2}
\end{equation}

Therefore, for \(P_N=|u_N\rangle\langle u_N|\otimes I\),

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
i_N^*U_{a,N}i_N&=I+\frac{d_a}{N},\\
(I-P_N)U_{a,N}i_N
&=\frac{\sqrt{N-1}}{N}\eta_N\otimes d_a.
\end{aligned}
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-3}
\end{equation}

where

\begin{equation}
\eta_N=\frac{Ne_0-\mathbf1_N}{\sqrt{N(N-1)}}.
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-4}
\end{equation}

From this the normalized difference reader is recovered:

\begin{equation}
\widehat\delta_{a,m}f=\eta_N\otimes u_{81}\otimes d_af,
\qquad
j_mf=u_N\otimes u_{81}\otimes f.
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-5}
\end{equation}

The action on any combination of generators, without eliminating their cross terms, is

\begin{equation}
\left\langle\sum_i\widehat\delta_{a_i,m}f_i,
                  \sum_j\widehat\delta_{b_j,m}g_j\right\rangle
=\sum_{i,j}\langle d_{a_i}f_i,d_{b_j}g_j\rangle.
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-6}
\end{equation}

In particular,

\begin{equation}
\widehat\delta_{a,m}^*\widehat\delta_{b,m}=d_a^*d_b,\qquad
j_m^*j_m=I,\qquad
j_m^*\widehat\delta_{a,m}=0.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-1}
\end{equation}

The last cross product comes from \(\langle u_N,\eta_N\rangle=0\). It is material information on the orientation of the modes, in addition to the two isolated norms.

\subsection{Two distinct refinements and their expectations}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-108}

\subsubsection{Uniform inclusion and projection of the last digit}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-110}

Write \(M=9N\) and \(\mathbb C^M=\mathbb C^N\otimes\mathbb C^9\). The inclusion and its adjoint are

\begin{equation}
\iota_Nx=x\otimes u_9,\qquad
E_N=I_N\otimes\langle u_9|,\qquad P_N^{\rm unif}=\iota_NE_N.
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-8}
\end{equation}

Acting in coordinates,

\begin{equation}
E_Nu_M=u_N,\qquad
E_N\eta_M=a_N\eta_N,\qquad
a_N=\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{9N-1}}.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-2}
\end{equation}

\begin{proof}
We have \(E_Ne_0^{(M)}=e_0^{(N)}/3\) and \(E_N\mathbf1_M=3\mathbf1_N\).
Substituting into \(\eta_M=(Me_0-\mathbf1_M)/\sqrt{M(M-1)}\)
yields \eqref{viii:gamma-add-eq-2}.
\end{proof}

Consequently,

\begin{equation}
(E_N\otimes I)\widehat\delta_{a,m+1}=a_N\widehat\delta_{a,m},
\qquad
(E_N\otimes I)j_{m+1}=j_m.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-3}
\end{equation}

All projected moments are determined:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\delta_{a,m+1}^*(P_N^{\rm unif}\otimes I)\widehat\delta_{b,m+1}
 &=a_N^2d_a^*d_b,\\
j_{m+1}^*(P_N^{\rm unif}\otimes I)j_{m+1}&=I,\\
j_{m+1}^*(P_N^{\rm unif}\otimes I)\widehat\delta_{b,m+1}&=0.
\end{aligned}
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-4}
\end{equation}

The complementary fraction of the carry is \(1-a_N^2=8N/(9N-1)\). For \(N=9\), the published fraction is \(1/10\), not \(1\), and its output remains a difference.

Here \(P_N^{\rm unif}\), which averages the last digit, is not confused with the projection onto the single global line \(u_M\): the latter annihilates \(\eta_M\) entirely.

\subsubsection{Helmert refinement}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-156}

The operator \(W_N\) is fixed by the orthonormal Helmert bases
of the two levels, ordered to preserve the uniform and carry
modes. It is the isometry that sends the first \(N\) vectors of the
basis at level \(N\) to the corresponding vectors of the basis at level \(M\).
In particular, it satisfies

\begin{equation}
W_Nu_N=u_M,\qquad W_N\eta_N=\eta_M,\qquad W_N^*W_N=I.
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-12}
\end{equation}

Thus, \(E_N^{\rm H}=W_N^*\), \(P_N^{\rm H}=W_NW_N^*\) give

\begin{equation}
E_N^{\rm H}j_{m+1}=j_m,\qquad
E_N^{\rm H}\widehat\delta_{a,m+1}=\widehat\delta_{a,m}.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-5}
\end{equation}

The uniform--carry cross product remains zero and that of two differences remains \(d_a^*d_b\). The refinement is therefore natural for both modes, but does not transform one into the other. Specifically, \(W_N\ne\iota_N\).

\subsubsection{Conjugation of expectations and preservation of the moments}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-174}

There is a unitary \(S_M\) on \(\mathbb C^M\) that takes \(\iota_N\mathbb C^N\) to \(W_N\mathbb C^N\), with \(W_N=S_M\iota_N\). It suffices to map the two orthonormal bases and complete the complements. Then

\begin{equation}
P_N^{\rm H}=S_MP_N^{\rm unif}S_M^*.
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-14}
\end{equation}

Simultaneously conjugating the state \(C\) and its projection,

\begin{equation}
(S_MC)^*(S_MP S_M^*)(S_MC)=C^*PC,\qquad
(S_MC)^*(S_MC)=C^*C.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-6}
\end{equation}

Neither moment changes. If only the projection is changed, in this case one passes from the fraction \(a_N^2\) to \(1\), but the analytic operator remains \(d_a\), not \(I\).

More generally, for any projection acting only on the finite history fiber, the moment of a single channel is
\(\langle\eta,P\eta\rangle d_a^*d_b\). That change of basis does not convert it into \(2I\). Any effective coupling between analytic channels is another operation and must be written down, not inferred from conjugation.

\subsection{Metric identities of the boundary and polar charts}

\subsubsection{Bidirectional boundary}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-197}

The bidirectional chart preserves the composition

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
K_2&=\mathbb C^{81}\otimes u_9^\perp,\\
\Delta_a&=j_{\rm APP}(\eta_9\otimes d_a),\\
\beta_0&=R_{\rm tw}(L^3+\varepsilon\nabla_x)FI_{Q_9}|_{K_2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-16}
\end{equation}

In this realization, \(j_{\rm APP}\) is the isometric inclusion
of the indicated fiber; \(\beta_0\) is injective and
\(\Lambda_{\rm tw}^{\#}\) is positive definite on the boundary
space. These are the conditions of the chart: the factors of the
composition are received with their compatible domains, and here that
injectivity is not deduced from a coincidence of dimensions.

With \(V_\beta=\beta_0(\beta_0^*\beta_0)^{-1/2}\) and \(U_{\rm tw}=(\Lambda_{\rm tw}^{\#})^{-1/2}V_\beta\),

\begin{equation}
G_a=(U_{\rm tw}\otimes I)\Delta_a,\qquad
G_a^*(\Lambda_{\rm tw}^{\#}\otimes I)G_b=d_a^*d_b.

\begin{proof}
The polar normalization gives \(V_\beta^*V_\beta=I\) and, therefore,

The identity \eqref{viii:gamma-add-eq-7} contains neither an expectation nor its value on \(G_a\). To transport a projection, it must be conjugated with that same isometry and its adjoint in the correct metric. Done this way, \eqref{viii:gamma-add-eq-6} preserves the preceding compressed moment: changing to the boundary does not create the second moment.

\subsubsection{Effective polar chart}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-220}

The polar realization through five octads provides the five-column
matrix \(T\), whose Gram and normalization are

\begin{equation}
T^*T=4I_5+\frac43J_5,\qquad
\widehat T=T(T^*T)^{-1/2},\qquad
F_{34}=(f_3,f_6,f_9,f_4,f_8).
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-18}
\end{equation}

The chart with its explicit spaces is

\begin{equation}
U=F_{34}\widehat T^*:
\operatorname{Ran}\widehat T\subset\mathbb C^{24}
\longrightarrow\operatorname{span}(f_3,f_6,f_9,f_4,f_8)\subset\mathbb C^{12}.
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-19}
\end{equation}

Para \(\eta=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1,-1)\),

\begin{equation}
U^*J_{34}U=\widehat T\eta\widehat T^*,\qquad
\widehat T^*U^*J_{34}U\widehat T=\eta.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-8}
\end{equation}

Here the five vectors of \(F_{34}\) are orthonormal and
\(F_{34}^*J_{34}F_{34}=\eta\). Substituting
\(U=F_{34}\widehat T^*\) and \(\widehat T^*\widehat T=I_5\)
proves the two equalities in \eqref{viii:gamma-add-eq-8}.
This is the typed expression of the equality with the diagonal matrix.
The five roles are effective; the identification of coordinates must
not be erased.

For the two polar evaluations,

\subsection{Difference between channel-by-channel reading and common transfer}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-259}

To fix all the summands used, let
\(\mathcal K_m=\mathbb C^{9^m}\otimes\mathbb C^{81}\otimes H\),
and let \(\zeta_+\), \(\zeta_-\) be unit vectors of the two
polar lines. The indices \(\ell\leq R\) run through the prime--power
labels \((p,k)\) with \(k\log p\leq R\), preserving
their weight \(w_\ell=(\log p)p^{-k/2}\). The columns are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J_{+,R}f={}&\Bigl(
 (\sqrt{w_\ell}\,\widehat\delta_{\ell,m}f)_{\ell\leq R},\,
 (\sqrt{\mu(a)}\,\widehat\delta_{a,m}f)_{a>0},\,
 \zeta_+b_+(f)\Bigr),\\
J_{-,R}f={}&\Bigl(
 \sqrt{-c_\Gamma}\,j_mf,\,
 (\sqrt{2w_\ell}\,j_mf)_{\ell\leq R},\,
 \zeta_-b_-(f)\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{viii:gamma-add-columnas-completas}
\end{equation}
The continuous component of the first column belongs to
\(L^2((0,\infty),da;\mathcal K_m)\); the remaining components
are gathered in the indicated orthogonal sums. The evaluations
\(b_\pm\) are those of \eqref{viii:gamma-add-eq-9}. Their common domain
is that of tests with finite gamma energy. The identity
\begin{equation}
\mathscr W_R(f,g)
=\langle J_{+,R}f,J_{+,R}g\rangle
 -\langle J_{-,R}f,J_{-,R}g\rangle
\label{viii:gamma-add-diferencia-columnas}
\end{equation}
is verified by expanding
\(\langle d_\ell f,d_\ell g\rangle
=2\langle f,g\rangle-\langle T_\ell f,g\rangle
-\langle f,T_\ell g\rangle\), using the gamma integral
and the polar identity \eqref{viii:gamma-add-eq-9}. Thus the three
channels and all cross terms are preserved.

Taking the explicit positive column \(\Xi_+\) as the encoder satisfies its first moment by calculation. Applying the uniform expectation afterwards yields in each prime channel

\begin{equation}
w_\ell a_N^2\langle d_\ell f,d_\ell g\rangle,
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-22}
\end{equation}

whereas the required negative row is

\begin{equation}
2w_\ell\langle f,g\rangle.
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-23}
\end{equation}

They are not the same identity. For example, choose \(R>\ell>0\) and real, nonnegative \(f\in\mathcal D_R\), with support overlapping its translation by \(\ell\). Then

\begin{equation}
\|d_\ell f\|^2
=2\|f\|^2-2\langle f,T_\ell f\rangle
<2\|f\|^2.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-10}
\end{equation}

No contractive projection applied solely to that channel can produce \(\sqrt2f\). For the Helmert refinement the factor \(a_N^2\) is eliminated, but \eqref{viii:gamma-add-eq-10} persists. This is a falsifier of the \textbf{channel-by-channel} identification, not a refutation of a common transfer that redistributes energy between the channels.

On the other hand, directly introducing \(j_mf\), its prime--scalar copies and \(b_-(f)\) as orthogonal summands of the encoder ensures its outputs, but adds their norms to the first moment. Maintaining the original first moment requires an explicit isometric redistribution. That redistribution is precisely the operation that the simultaneous identification of the two moments must exhibit.

The invariance of the cyclic subspace under \(P\), by itself, does not determine the value of \(PCf\) either; it only guarantees that this value remains in the subspace.

\subsection{Inverse of the gamma tail and reconstruction of all rows}
\label{viii:gamma-add-sub-288}

Here there is indeed an explicit action on every generator and every linear combination. Let

\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=\frac{e^{-a/2}}{1-e^{-2a}},\quad
A_R=[R+1,R+2],\quad \nu_R=\int_{A_R}\mu(a)\,da>0,
\quad M_R=\mathbf1_{(-R/2,R/2)}.
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-25}
\end{equation}

For the already fixed sign \(d_a=T_a-I\), we define the cotrace

\begin{equation}
\epsilon_m=\langle\eta_{9^m}|\otimes\langle u_{81}|\otimes M_R,
\qquad
\mathcal R^-_{m,R}F
=-\nu_R^{-1}\int_{A_R}\sqrt{\mu(a)}\,\epsilon_m F(a)\,da.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-11}
\end{equation}

The minus sign disappears if \(I-T_a\) is used, which is the alternative convention of the tail reader. For \(f\in\mathcal D_R\) and \(a>R\), \(M_RT_af=0\). Consequently,

\begin{equation}
\mathcal R^-_{m,R}
\bigl(a\mapsto\sqrt{\mu(a)}\widehat\delta_{a,m}f\bigr)=f.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-12}
\end{equation}
\label{viii:gamma-add:cola-lector}

Moreover, \(\|\mathcal R^-_{m,R}\|\le\nu_R^{-1/2}\) by Cauchy--Schwarz. No individual translation is inverted, nor is Weil positivity used.

Write

\begin{equation}
S_Rf=\left(\sqrt{-c_\Gamma}\,j_mf,\,
              (\sqrt{2w_\ell}\,j_mf)_{\ell\le R}\right),
\quad B_-f=\zeta_-b_-(f).
\label{viii:gamma-add-display-28}
\end{equation}

The specific common row, defined on the complete positive ambient space, is

\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_R^{\rm tail}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&S_R\mathcal R^-_{m,R}&0\\
0&B_-\mathcal R^-_{m,R}&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-13}
\end{equation}

From \eqref{viii:gamma-add-eq-12},

\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_R^{\rm tail}J_{+,R}f=J_{-,R}f
\quad\hbox{for every }f\in\mathcal D_R.
\label{viii:gamma-add-eq-14}
\end{equation}

This action contains the scalar, prime--power and polar rows, with their constants and orientations intact. For any pair \(f,g\),

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\langle\mathcal C_R^{\rm tail}J_+f,\mathcal C_R^{\rm tail}J_+g\rangle

\end{equation}

The bound fixes the type, not proving a norm less than or equal to one.
