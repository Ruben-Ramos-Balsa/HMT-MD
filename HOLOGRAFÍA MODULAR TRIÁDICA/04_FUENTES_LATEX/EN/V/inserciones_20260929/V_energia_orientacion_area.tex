\subsection{Recoverable energy within a fixed area distribution}
\label{ins29:v:orientacion}

The tritic boundary distinguishes the information carried by total
occupation from that carried by its sectorial origin. We use the two sets
of eighteen links, their occupations \(0,1,2\), the \(90/120\) weights,
and the thermal section constructed earlier. Write
\[
 X=N_{90},\qquad Y=N_{120},\qquad N=X+Y,\quad M=X-Y,
 \qquad d=30\Delta_{\rm mod}>0.
\]
Let \(\epsilon=k_BT>0\) be the energy scale of the specified realization.
Its Hamiltonian and reference state are
\[
 H=\epsilon d(3X+4Y),\qquad
 \rho_0=Z^{-1}e^{-H/\epsilon}.
\]
The previously constructed area operator is
\(\widehat{\mathcal A}=a_{\rm b}N\), with
\(a_{\rm b}=\gamma a_0L_\alpha^2>0\) fixed throughout the protocol.
Define \(W\) as the exchange of the two sets of links, retaining every
occupation. This permutation reverses \(M\) and leaves \(N\) invariant.
The complementary occupation reversal \(n\mapsto2-n\), treated earlier,
retains its own function and is a distinct operation.

\begin{proposition}[Energy response invisible to the area reading]
\label{ins29:v:respuesta}
The state \(\rho_1=W\rho_0W^*\) has the same full area distribution
and the same von Neumann entropy as \(\rho_0\), whereas
\[
 \Delta E=\operatorname{Tr}[H(\rho_1-\rho_0)]
 =\epsilon\mathcal J(d)>0,
\]
\[
 \mathcal J(d)=D(\rho_1\Vert\rho_0)
 =18d\,[\mu(3d)-\mu(4d)],\qquad
 \mu(a)=\frac{e^{-a}+2e^{-2a}}{1+e^{-a}+e^{-2a}}.
\]
The inverse exchange recovers exactly \(\Delta E\); this is the maximum
energy recoverable by a unitary transformation of \(\rho_1\), for the
fixed Hamiltonian \(H\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since \(WNW^*=N\), every spectral projector of the area commutes with
\(W\), preserving its probability. Unitarity preserves the spectrum
and entropy of the state. In the occupation basis, write \(p_0,p_1\)
for the probabilities. Exchange gives
\[
 \log\frac{p_1}{p_0}=-dM,\qquad
 \langle M\rangle_{p_1}=-\langle M\rangle_{p_0},\qquad
 \langle M\rangle_{p_0}=18[\mu(3d)-\mu(4d)].
\]
The weighted sum of the logarithm proves the formula for
\(D(\rho_1\Vert\rho_0)\). Moreover,
\(\mu'(a)=-\operatorname{Var}_a(n)<0\) for the three positive weights,
so \(\mathcal J(d)>0\).
The Gibbs identity, together with equality of the entropies, gives
\(\Delta E=\epsilon D(\rho_1\Vert\rho_0)\).
For any unitary \(V\), the state \(V\rho_1V^*\) retains that same
entropy and satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}[H(V\rho_1V^*-\rho_0)]
 =\epsilon D(V\rho_1V^*\Vert\rho_0)\geq0.
\]
The choice \(V=W^*\) attains equality and proves maximality.
\end{proof}

The area distribution retains every multiplicity of \(N\), whereas
the Hamiltonian distinguishes its allocations \((X,Y)\). The excess
energy is located precisely in that distinction. When the corresponding
horizon realization is used, its scale is
\(\epsilon=\hbar c/(2\pi R)\). Thus, action converts \(\mathcal J(d)\)
into an energy response at fixed radius. The area operator of this
preparation and the geometric area of a horizon with backreaction retain
their respective domains.

\subsubsection{Conditional recovery and reduced functional}

The information within each fibre admits an exact formulation. On the
finite configuration space, let \(q\) be a surjective extractor,
\(p_{0,q}=q_*p_0\), and \(p_q=q_*p\). For a distribution \(r\) on the
extractor, define
\[
 (\mathcal R_0r)(x)
 =r(q(x))\frac{p_0(x)}{p_{0,q}(q(x))}.
\]
This recovery map satisfies \(q_*\mathcal R_0=I\). Its conditional
distribution retains the thermal weights within each fibre.

\begin{proposition}[Free energy of information within a fibre]
\label{ins29:v:restitucion}
For \(F_T(p)=\sum_xp(x)H(x)+\epsilon\sum_xp(x)\log p(x)\),
\[
 F_T(p)-F_T(\mathcal R_0p_q)
 =\epsilon D(p\Vert\mathcal R_0p_q).
\]
Thus, \(\mathcal R_0p_q\) is the unique free-energy minimizer among
distributions with marginal \(p_q\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the support of \(p\), the factorization
\[
 \frac{p(x)}{p_0(x)}=
 \frac{p_q(q(x))}{p_{0,q}(q(x))}
 \frac{p(x)}{(\mathcal R_0p_q)(x)}
\]
gives, upon taking logarithms and summing,
\(D(p\Vert p_0)=D(p_q\Vert p_{0,q})+
D(p\Vert\mathcal R_0p_q)\).
The identity \(F_T(p)=\epsilon D(p\Vert p_0)-\epsilon\log Z\),
together with the same formula for \(\mathcal R_0p_q\), proves the
statement. The divergence vanishes precisely when its arguments
coincide; the convention is \(0\log0=0\).
\end{proof}

For \(q=N\), write
\(g_{18}(x)=[t^x](1+t+t^2)^{18}\) for the tritic multiplicity already
constructed. The effective Hamiltonian of the area reading is
\[
 \frac{H_N(n)}\epsilon=
 \frac{7d}{2}n-\log\!\sum_{x+y=n}
 g_{18}(x)g_{18}(y)e^{d(x-y)/2}.
\]
This expression follows by summing \(e^{-H/\epsilon}\) over the fibre.
The sum is invariant under reversal of the sign of its oriented term,
by exchange of \(x\) and \(y\). For the preceding preparation,
\(p_{1,N}=p_{0,N}\) and \(\mathcal R_0p_{1,N}=p_0\): all excess
free energy resides in the information omitted by this area reading.
By contrast, retaining \((N,M)\) fixes the energy of each fibre and makes
its conditional distribution uniform. The result specifies which
observables suffice to recover this collection and its thermal response.
